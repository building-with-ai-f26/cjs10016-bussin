## Platform

ios

## Stack

SwiftUI + MapKit, iOS 18 minimum, Xcode project generated by XcodeGen (`ios/`). No third-party app dependencies except the PostHog SDK. The HTML prototype in `docs/prototype/` is a design reference, not the product. Decided with the user Nov 9, 2026 (spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-bussin-ios-app-design.md`).

## Users

NYU students first (confirmed Nov 10, 2026). They ride NYU's free shuttles between Washington Square, Brooklyn (Tandon/MetroTech), Langone, and points east, usually standing at or walking to a stop, phone in one hand, deciding right now: wait, walk, or catch a different route. Faculty and staff who ride benefit too, but product decisions optimize for students.

## Product Purpose

Show where every NYU shuttle is, when it reaches each stop, and which shuttle beats walking to a destination. It exists because Passio GO, the app NYU points riders to, shows buses on a map but answers none of those questions. Semester success (user-confirmed): traction beyond the class — shared in NYU groups, aiming for 100+ installs before the Dec 14 sponsor memo, with PostHog proving real usage.

## Positioning

The only NYU-specific shuttle app with real arrival times, trip planning against walking, and per-bus ridership awareness — built on the same live feed Passio itself uses, so its data can't be worse than the incumbent's. Longer game: logged ridership-by-route-by-hour data gives NYU Transportation evidence for route changes no one else has collected.

## Operating Context

- Used outdoors, in motion, often in a hurry; glanceable answers beat dense screens.
- The hub stop is 715 Broadway; seven lettered routes (A, B, C, E, F, G, W), with W weekends-only and some routes down outside service hours.
- Incumbent: Passio GO (universal transit app, no ETAs at stop level, no routing).
- Built inside NYU Stern's "Building with AI" course: weekly build logs on the product page (`docs/index.html`), sponsor memo due Dec 14, 2026. Faster private cadence: the builder uses the app himself as soon as the planner works.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Live data from Passio GO's public feed (`passio3.com/www/mapGetData.php`, NYU system 1007, no auth): routes, stops, bus positions every few seconds, riders aboard (`paxLoad`/`totalCap`), per-bus heading, staleness flags, and Passio's own suspended-routes list (`excludedRoutesID`).
- **Unconfirmed:** whether Passio permits third-party use of the feed. Standing risk; app stays free and attributes the source.
- Scope: NYU shuttles only. Not a mobile wallet or ID, not a general navigation app.
- Distribution: TestFlight (Apple Developer enrollment in progress), App Store later.
- Analytics: PostHog, key kept in gitignored `ios/Secrets.xcconfig`; app fully functional without it.
- Roadmap (PRD "Future Features"): service alerts incl. holiday schedules, Campus Safety reporting from the bus screen, crowding indicator, alternate app icons.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Bussin** (final).
- Logo direction (user-pinned): NYC-signage-inspired lettering. Three finalists in `logo/round-4-shortlist.html` — Mosaic, Station sign, Bus LED display — final pick open. The station-lamp icon is reserved as a future alternate app icon, not the main identity.
- Visual direction for the app: native Apple feel modeled on Apple Maps (user-pinned since Build 1); route colors come from the Passio feed and are shared with NYU's own materials.

## Evidence on Hand

- Interactive prototype: `docs/prototype/index.html` (live on GitHub Pages) — the approved design reference for every core screen.
- Real feed snapshots: `tools/prototype/data/passio-stops.json`, `passio-routes.json`; test fixtures planned under `ios/BussinTests/Fixtures/`.
- Live-feed findings (verified Nov 8–9, 2026): open feed, 13 active vehicles observed, `paxLoad` real-looking (56/64 at rush, 28/64 mid-afternoon) but unvalidated — do not build claims on it until a week of collector data checks out.
- No testimonials, press, or usage numbers yet; nothing may be fabricated. First usage evidence arrives via PostHog after TestFlight.

## Product Principles

1. **Answer the rider's question first.** Where's my bus, when does it get here, which one do I take — before any other feature.
2. **Never lie about data.** Simulated, stale, or estimated values are labeled as such; a wrong ETA presented confidently is worse than none.
3. **Native, not wrapped.** The app should be indistinguishable in feel from Apple's own maps and transit surfaces.
4. **Data earns influence.** Collect ridership evidence quietly and continuously; the route pitch to NYU is part of the product.
5. **Students first.** Tone, defaults, and feature order serve a student catching a shuttle between classes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Deliberate roadmap item, not a current commitment (user decision, Nov 10, 2026): VoiceOver labels, Dynamic Type, and contrast work are planned after the core app ships, not during it. Future work should avoid choices that make that retrofit harder than it needs to be.
