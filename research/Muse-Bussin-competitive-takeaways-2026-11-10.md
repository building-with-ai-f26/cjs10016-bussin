# Bussin Competitive Research — Takeaways

Compiled 10 Nov 2026 from four research passes: Passio GO public sentiment, Facebook posts, Citymapper vs TripShot, and the wider competitor landscape. All values are as reported by the sources; hedges and gaps are kept in place. Sources at the end of each section.

---

## 1. Passio GO — the incumbent

### The ratings paradox

Headline ratings look fine, but the written reviews and campus press are mostly negative.

- App Store: 4.6/5, 11K ratings (v6.1.2). Google Play: 4.1/5, 1,676 ratings (updated 17 Aug 2026).
- Historically much worse: 1.4 stars in Sept 2022 (Harvard Crimson Flyby), 1.5 in Sept 2023.
- Most recent written reviews found: Jean Kuhn 5★, 18 Oct 2026 and Ariel Barton 1★, 14 Oct 2026 (Google Play via AppBrain); spromine 1★, 1 Oct 2026 and bizzarer.io (Chapman) 1★, 18 Sept 2026 (App Store via iOSAppGallery); Lauren Tucker 1★, 15 July 2026 (Google Play).

### The 7 complaint themes, ranked by recurrence

1. **Inaccurate / jumping ETAs and lagging GPS.** "Bus arrival times will jump up down and sideways, busses will freeze on the map and jump forwards, or even not appear at all" (WorldsApps). ETA swings "14 minutes, to 3 minutes, back to 14, and then to 50 minutes" (WorldsApps).
2. **Buses disappear / empty map / false "service has not started."** "constantly says service has not started while I am staring at a bus in service drive past me" (Lauren Tucker, Google Play, 15 July 2026). Salve Regina op-ed, Nov 2026: students open the app to "an empty map."
3. **Crashes / freezes / won't load.** "Every time that I try to open the app, the first time, it always crashes" (Harvard student, Crimson, 1 Apr 2026). "Will not load any routes or buses 80% of the time" (Google Play, 4 June 2026).
4. **Confusing / cluttered UI, painful route selection.** "sort of a joke among Quad students that Passio GO! is just really poorly designed" (Crimson).
5. **No origin-to-destination trip planning.** "The app needs to actually take into account people wanting it to be able to get places, not just tracking buses" (Google Play, Jan 2025).
6. **Only the next bus shown, no tomorrow / full schedule.**
7. **Agency / route saving broken, wrong / outdated routes and stops.** SCAD students "kicked out of the 'agency'" and unable to rejoin (App Store snippet). Rochester Campus Times, 5 Feb 2024: wrong route via Mt Hope Ave, a stop shown in a parking lot, inbound/outbound stops merged.

### What users praise

Live map and ETAs *when* accurate, seeing all routes at once, free with no registration, bus capacity info, and one iPhone user's stop alarm. A few say it has improved (now shows three bus times).

### Schools that left

- **Harvard replaced Passio GO with Citymapper on 1 July 2026** after years of complaints and a review of 20+ vendors (Crimson, 11 July 2026).
- **Rochester / RIT moved to TripShot** ("definitely better than [PassioGO!]").
- **UChicago survey, June 2025:** about half of students "somewhat or very dissatisfied."

### NYU-specific evidence

No NYU newspaper article or verifiable r/nyu thread was found. Evidence is two student UX case studies:

- **Schron, Fall 2021 (diary study, n=5):** all 5 had quit the app, all prefer Google Sheets, all prefer the mobile site over the app. "No place to put destination… Doesn't say how long until shuttles leave 721 Broadway." "easier to keep the Google Sheet schedule open on Safari than use the app, which makes it useless."
- **Duorfan (12 NYU interviews):** confusing interface, no shuttle-vs-subway comparison. "Some days I wait over 30 minutes for a shuttle, only to realize I could have taken the train." Alerts "practically invisible."

NYU complaints skew to UX and fragmentation rather than replacement campaigns; other schools skew to accuracy, crashes, and replacement. NYU evidence is small (n=5, n=12) and from 2021–2023.

### Features users ask for

Origin-to-destination routing and best-option comparison (including subway/walking and total trip time); more than one upcoming bus per stop plus tomorrow / full-week schedules; driver break / clock-out info; an Apple Watch app; persistent favourites / saved routes and agencies; reliable alarms / delay alerts; stop order per route; working in-app feedback. Note: Passio's own site already claims favourites, notifications, walking directions, and ETAs — the gap is claim vs working reality.

### Reliability notes

GPS lag and freezing, buses disappearing, crashes on first open and on opening notifications, outdated/wrong schedules and routes, false "service has not started." No login is required, so login failures are NOT a theme; the adjacent failure is agency selection (kicked out / losing an agency). Some failures are partly hardware / agency-side (Salve shuttles lacking trackers; Rochester equipment issues).

### Could not verify (Passio GO pass)

Reddit (site search returned no results; no r/nyu or r/rutgers threads found) and X/Twitter (no complaint posts found). Rutgers' reported switch to TripShot is unconfirmed (single index source). An anecdote aggregator claiming ~60% inaccurate / ~50% crashes has no methodology and was not cited as fact. App Store review quotes for SCAD / fake-review allegation / route-planning are search-index snippets only (the direct App Store open showed no review text); the fake-review claim is one reviewer's allegation, not fact.

## 3. Citymapper vs TripShot — where they beat Passio GO

Headline: both replacements beat Passio GO mainly on (1) real origin-to-destination trip planning and (2) more accurate / less crash-prone live ETAs. But the gains are user/admin testimony, not independent measurement, and in every switch the app change coincided with a new operator and/or new GPS hardware. Citymapper is the bigger rider leap (multimodal city app); TripShot is the bigger rider-plus-operator leap (full campus fleet platform).

### Citymapper

Harvard replaced Passio GO with Citymapper on 1 July 2026; also used at UChicago.

- **Features:** multimodal A→B planning (bus/subway/train/ferry/cabs/rideshare/bikeshare/e-scooters/walking, ranked by speed/price); GO step-by-step with get-off alert, best carriage/exit, lock-screen/watch; live map + arrival countdowns; disruption alerts + automatic rerouting; saved Home/Work/stops and favourite-line subscriptions; offline saved trips + metro/bus maps; step-free routing (27 cities per secondary reviews — not officially verified).
- **Harvard rollout:** Harvard Transportation, Aug 2026 — live map, countdowns, Evening Van/M2/MBTA/Bluebikes in one app; 20+ vendors reviewed. UChicago: UGo Shuttles, RideSmart, CTA, Metra, Divvy; buses updated every minute.
- **Coverage:** 400+ major cities incl. NYC (MTA data) — directly relevant to NYU. Owned by Via since Mar 2023.
- **Pricing:** free core; CLUB now mainly ad-free — £1.49/mo or £9.99/yr, UK App Store; US $1.49/$9.99 reported Aug 2024, may have changed. Campus/agency price not published.
- **Student verdict (Crimson, 4 Nov 2026):** Jin '29 — "where do you want to go, we'll get you there" vs Passio "relatively unclear"; Sheth '28 — sorts by arrival time, easier to compare; Xu '29 — more accurate timetable/GPS, "a big improvement… It's not perfect."
- **Limits:** overwhelming / campus-unfocused — Xue '30: "20 different buses… a while to scroll to the M2"; Kang '29: "I want Citymapper to be just for Harvard." No fleet/admin tools, no on-demand booking, no capacity, no reservations.
- **Caveat:** an unofficial student project claims Harvard's tracker behind Citymapper is a Ride Systems instance at shuttle.harvard.edu, so the accuracy gain may be the backend, not Citymapper alone.

### TripShot

Rochester & RIT, fall 2025; Rutgers, 1 Jul 2026; Emory, 21 Sep 2026.

- **Features:** True-Time "up-to-the-second, GPS-based" ETAs (vendor claim; GPS every 1–2 sec in a Mountain View case study); Advanced Trip Planner A→B; full schedules + stop-by-stop route detail; per-route/stop push notifications for arrival/departure/delay; favourites; live passenger capacity via APC counts; optional On Demand / SafeRide booking; Reservations; Bluetooth Boarding + Wallet ticketing; micromobility; web tracker at <school>.tripshot.com; admin dashboards (SLA, OTP, GPS playback/history to the second, ridership analytics, flexible scheduling, e.g. sick-driver changes); deploy "in as little as a few days"; ISO 27001.
- **Ratings:** Google Play 4.69★ / 1,150 ratings, updated 12 Oct 2026; rider app free.
- **Rochester/RIT testimony (Campus Times, 15 Sep 2025):** Galvin — "definitely better than [PassioGO!]" — Passio "would just crash on me… at least this one opens"; Cui — "UI… better than Passio GO!, but the actual function… just similar"; admin Chodak — substitute buses not yet equipped, integration due end Fall 2025. RIT Reporter — "more accurate… PassioGo had issues displaying the live location."
- **Northwestern** lists favourites, trip planner, alerts, live capacity — but with sign-up friction (service name + university-email verification).
- **Caveats:** Michigan Tech Lode, 3 Nov 2026 — a route change with no message in TripShot, a student missed the bus; Play reviews for other deployments report repeated 15-min delay notices and cancelled shuttles that "just disappear." Per-organisation only (no city-wide subway/bike); no offline mode found.
- **Pricing:** rider app free; agency pricing is quote-only ("Speak With Our Sales Team") — no public price found.

### Ranked head-to-head (for a student at the stop)

**Citymapper beats Passio GO on:** (1) A→B multimodal planning/comparison, (2) more accurate sortable ETAs, (3) GO guidance / get-off alert, (4) disruption alerts / rerouting, (5) saved places / line subscriptions, (6) offline saved trips/maps, (7) step-free routing.

**TripShot beats Passio GO on:** (1) more accurate live location/ETAs + an app that opens vs crashes, (2) trip planner + full/future schedules, (3) working per-stop delay notifications, (4) working favourites, (5) live capacity, (6) on-demand/SafeRide + reservations, (7) operator dashboard, (8) web tracker.

### Where Passio GO still beats either

No registration at all ("run Passio GO without any registration, use for free") vs TripShot email sign-up and Citymapper ads (Passio listed ad-free on Play); narrow single-purpose UI vs Citymapper's city-wide overload and TripShot's wallet/parking/micromobility clutter; passenger counting is a Passio strength (TripShot matches via APCs; Citymapper lacks it); small-agency/white-label coverage Citymapper (400+ major cities only) will not reach. Agency cost: unverifiable for all three.

### Could not verify (Citymapper / TripShot pass)

Agency pricing (all three); independent ETA/GPS accuracy benchmarks; Citymapper step-free city count (27, secondary only); definitive US CLUB price; TripShot offline mode; any NYU-specific feed detail.

## 4. The wider competitor landscape

**Bottom line:** Passio GO's most dangerous competitors for Bussin are not the big operator platforms — they are (1) **XRadar**, a student-built native-iOS tracker that already claims NYU coverage and directly attacks Passio's ETA/UI weaknesses, and (2) **Transit app**, the consumer transit app NYU students already open for the subway/MTA, which sets the UX and ETA-accuracy bar and could absorb the shuttle if NYU ever published a GTFS-realtime feed. Everything else is either a legacy campus vendor with the same flaws as Passio (TransLoc, DoubleMap, GMV Syncromatics, Ride Systems — now discontinued), a general NYC transit app that does not carry the NYU shuttle (Moovit, Google Maps, MTA app), or an operator backend that is not a student-facing threat (Swiftly, TripSpark, RouteMatch, Ecolane).

### Ranking by relevance to Bussin / NYU

1. **XRadar** — direct, NYU-claiming, student-built
2. **Transit app** — NYC default, ETA/UX benchmark
3. **Moovit / Google Maps / MTA app (tied)** — general NYC trip planning students already use
4. **TransLoc** — incumbent vendor, Georgia Tech precedent + OnDemand
5. **Navgic / Destination** — small NYU-specific indie trackers
6. **Via** — on-demand / Safe Ride analogue
7. **Swiftly** — backend that could power a rival's ETAs
8. **GMV Syncromatics** — white-label campus apps, same flaws as Passio
9. **DoubleMap** — legacy, same disappearing-bus complaints
10. **TripSpark MyRide** — capable rider app, no campus footprint found
11. **RouteMatch / Ecolane** — operator-only, paratransit-focused
12. **Ride Systems** — discontinued 31 Aug 2025, removed from Google Play Oct 2025

### 4.1 XRadar — watch first

- **What it is:** student-built native-iOS tracker (ex-RURadar, X Radar Inc.). Store listing claims Rutgers, NYU, Columbia, UGA, Alabama; the homepage (opened 10 Nov 2026) features only Rutgers, UGA, Alabama, UConn — **NYU coverage in the store listing needs in-app verification.**
- **Features:** machine-learned ETAs claimed 1–2 min, stops in order, live capacity on tap, favourite routes, alerts, class-schedule route suggestions, widgets / Live Activities.
- **Ratings:** App Store 4.79/5, 48 ratings (WorldsApps) / 4.5, 60 (AppRecs) — small sample.
- **Reviews:** "clears Passio GO by a mile"; "a lot nicer than Passio GO… you can even favorite routes."
- **Origin story (Rutgers Alumni):** built because Passio GO "hid all the important buttons in a hamburger menu, the map's zooming gesture is unresponsive… and important features — like the order of stops along a specific route — are completely missing."
- **Takeaway:** the only rival that is (a) student-built, (b) native-iOS and ETA-obsessed, (c) already claiming NYU + Columbia, and (d) rated 4.79 with reviews explicitly comparing itself favourably to Passio GO. Its weakness is scale (48–60 ratings, iOS-first, homepage does not currently feature NYU) — Bussin can out-execute it on NYU depth, Android/web, and Safe Ride integration, but must match its ETA accuracy and favourites on day one.

### 4.2 Transit app — watch second

- **What it is:** consumer transit app by Transit Inc. 1,000+ cities, 9M+ monthly users, 200 official agency partners across four countries (Metro Magazine, Nov 2026). Campus model: "Royale for Agencies" — e.g. UMD's Shuttle-UM is integrated so only students/staff see campus routes, with gamification and co-branding; CU Boulder gives all affiliates free Royale (Swiftly blog, 8 Sep 2026).
- **Rider features:** A-to-B trip planning across bus/subway/train/bike/scooter; GO step-by-step navigator with departure alarms and get-off alerts; live vehicle tracking; favourites (lines, stops, destinations, one-tap trips); service alerts; offline schedules/maps/planner; crowdsourced crowding / Rate-My-Ride (~500,000 feedback items/day); in-house ML ETA model that combines agency data + GO crowdsourcing, detects detours and temporary stops, and works underground via motion sensors.
- **Operator features:** agency partnership / Royale sponsorship; rider-feedback analytics; ETA Accuracy Benchmark built with Swiftly.
- **vs Passio GO:** beats Passio on every listed weakness *except shuttle coverage* — real trip planning (Passio has none / broken transfers), favourites that work, ML ETAs designed to fix jumping predictions, GO crowdsourcing that shows where a bus actually is "even when official data is late or missing." Falls short for Bussin in that **no evidence was found that Transit carries the NYU Passio shuttle at all** — it depends on agency GTFS-realtime feeds, which Passio/NYU do not appear to publish openly. Also: Transit Royale paywall — free users are limited (e.g. Ole Miss, Oct 2026: $5/mo or $25/yr for full route maps/schedules; only the three closest departures free) unless the agency/university sponsors it; recent Play reviews also report crashes and a confusing UI redesign.
- **Pricing / ratings:** free to download; Royale $5/mo or $25/yr where not sponsored. Google Play 4.6, ~400K reviews; App Store rating not separately verified in this pass.
- **NYU / NYC:** heavily used in NYC for MTA subway/bus; NYU Steinhardt's own guide recommends Passio GO for shuttles and Google Maps/Citymapper for navigation, not Transit for the shuttle. No NYU shuttle deployment found.
- **Takeaway:** the app NYU students already trust for everything *except* the shuttle. If NYU/Passio ever expose GTFS-realtime, Transit absorbs the shuttle overnight and Bussin's moat narrows to NYU-specific UX. The Royale paywall ($5/mo or $25/yr unsponsored) is Bussin's opening: Bussin is free and shuttle-only, no paywall, no MTA clutter. Copy its GO crowdsourcing + ML ETA ideas.

### 4.3 Moovit / Google Maps / MTA app — the multi-modal layer

**Moovit** (Intel-owned): 930M+ users, 3,400+ cities. Journey planner combining all modes, real-time GPS arrivals, step-by-step live directions with get-off alerts, service alerts, user reports, favourite lines/stations/places, bike routes + docking, AR wayfinder, ticket purchase in select cities, offline PDF maps. Beats Passio on planning, favourites, alerts, and live guidance — the exact gaps Bussin targets. Falls short: heavy ads (Play reviewers call full-screen ads "unusable"), GPS lag reported, no campus-shuttle features (no vehicle capacity, no per-agency white label), and **no evidence it carries the NYU shuttle**. Free; in-app purchases $0.99–$14.99 (secondary aggregator, indicative). Google Play 3.9, ~1.5M reviews; App Store (US) 4.6, 61K ratings — the large Play/iOS gap is real in the listings, not an average.

**Google Maps:** preinstalled / ubiquitous; transit directions in 25,000+ cities per secondary guides. A-to-B multi-modal planning, route comparison, walking directions, real-time departures from GTFS-realtime, service alerts, crowding estimates in select cities, offline maps, fare/OMNY info. Beats Passio on planning, ubiquity, and clarity; falls short on live shuttle specifics — no live vehicle map for NYU shuttles, no capacity, no stop-level shuttle alerts, and NYC reviews say real-time "can lag behind the MTA app during major disruptions" and may ignore planned-work reroutes. A complement to Passio today (students use both), not a replacement. Free. NYU's own graduate guide lists Google Maps as a "best transit app for navigation" — alongside, not instead of, Passio GO for shuttles. No evidence NYU shuttle live data is in Google Maps.

**MTA app (official, ex-MYmta):** relaunched 25 Mar 2026 with "more accurate, higher-frequency" real-time data, live bus location markers, crowding, transfer ETAs, favourites, trip planner, accessibility mode, 24/7 live-agent chat. Beats Passio on NYC real-time accuracy (direct feed), crowding, favourites, and trip planning — for MTA services. Falls short: Google Play rating only 3.0 (7,436 ratings, AppBrain), with complaints that bus times are schedule-only/inaccurate vs the Bus Time website; and it covers **MTA only — no NYU shuttle**. Free. Takeaway: the app every NYU student already has for subway/bus; irrelevant to the shuttle itself, relevant as the multi-modal layer Bussin must interoperate with (e.g. shuttle → subway transfers).

### 4.4 TransLoc — the displaced incumbent that once displaced Passio

- **What it is:** founded 2004 by NC State students for the Wolfline; acquired by Ford Smart Mobility (Jan 2018); Modaxo acquired it from Ford (1 Mar 2022). Now the parent of the old Ride Systems / DoubleMap family. Users include Georgia Tech (Stinger/Stingerette), Emory, Kennesaw State, SCAD, Univ. of Pittsburgh SafeRider (OnDemand), plus many city systems.
- **Features:** trip planner (A-to-B), favourite stops, instant arrivals, live map, OnDemand/microtransit booking in the same app, payment where available. New Rider App (2025): unified fixed-route + OnDemand, vehicle capacity, suggested routes, ETAs, alerts, multilingual, WCAG 2.0 AA. Operator side: fixed-route + OnDemand dispatch, automated passenger counting, stop announcements, reporting suite.
- **vs Passio GO:** beats Passio on paper on trip planning, favourites, and — uniquely — on-demand night service in one app (the Safe Ride use case NYU splits across apps). Falls short in practice: Google Play 3.2 (1,804 ratings), with reviews saying it "only ever works when you don't need it", is slow, and fails to track buses; Brownsville and Laguna Beach users in 2025–26 called the new Rider App slower and worse at tracking than Ride Systems / old TransLoc. It shares Passio's core failure modes rather than fixing them.
- **Displacement:** **Georgia Tech, July 2022 — TransLoc replaced Passio GO**, consolidating fixed-route Stinger + on-demand Stingerette in one app. The only verified reverse. Conversely, Passio displaced TransLoc at Rutgers (2 Aug 2023), UConn (Dec 2021), NC State (12 Aug 2024), SMU, UChicago (2024), and Longwood Collective, Boston (2 Jan 2026). Rutgers has since moved on again to TripShot as its official tracker (date not verified here).
- **Pricing:** free to riders; agency pricing not public (quote).
- **Takeaway:** the vendor most likely to re-win a campus contract (Georgia Tech precedent) and the only one with fixed-route + on-demand in one app. Low ratings (3.2) limit its threat as a *student* choice; its threat is procurement, not the App Store.

### 4.5 Student-built & university-built trackers — the real Bussin peer group

These matter more than most vendors, because they are built the way Bussin is: by students, on top of the incumbent's feed, fixing exactly Passio's weaknesses.

- **RUBus (Rutgers student, PWA):** recalculates ETAs server-side from Passio position/capacity data "down to the second"; building-to-building routing; chatbot for arrivals / leave-time; no install (PWA). 80,000+ unique users Fall 2025; 7M requests Sep 2025. Rutgers-only — **the template for Bussin.**
- **Destination (Shafil Alam, indie):** one app for every system; data from Passio GO + TransLoc APIs. 800+ systems incl. NYU, Stanford, Harvard. v2 (May 2026) added NYU support. Rating, pricing, and current NYU data quality not verified.
- **Navgic (Navgic LLC):** campus map + buildings + walking directions + live bus tracker + Veo scooters in one app; free, no account; web + iOS + Android. Rutgers, NYU, Stony Brook, Binghamton, Kent State, UGA. 35 installs, no ratings yet (AppBrain). **NYU campus map & live bus tracker.**
- **PennTransit Mobile (Univ. of Pennsylvania, university-built):** advanced origin-to-destination trip planner, stop-by-stop schedules, intelligent notifications, on-demand live view — the university-built gold standard. 4.83, 230 ratings (AppBrain). Penn only.
- **NYU Mobile / Safe NYU (NYU official):** official app already includes real-time shuttle tracking + Safe Ride features (NYU Law handbook).
- **NYU Bus Tracker (open-source student, GitHub tpalsulich):** merged TransLoc data + NYU schedules, full timetables — predates Passio. Legacy / stale (TransLoc-era).

Caveat for all of these: they sit *on top of* Passio/TransLoc data, so an upstream GPS dropout can still make a bus disappear; their edge is ETA maths, UI, and favourites, not the vehicle feed. RUBus is explicit about this (uses Passio positioning/capacity, calculates everything else itself).

### 4.6 Operator backends and on-demand — not student-facing threats, but relevant

- **Swiftly:** not a rider app — Transit Data Platform for 190+ agencies (UMBC, Univ. of Maryland, WMATA, Maryland MTA, SEPTA, CTtransit). Multi-source GPS, prediction engine (vendor claim: 20–50% more accurate than other providers; SEPTA +26%, CTtransit +24%, Pierce Transit +27% prediction accuracy), Auto-Assigner (matches vehicles to trips when hardware/login fails — directly addresses disappearing buses), disruption-aware predictions (no predictions for cancelled trips/closed stops), Service Adjustments + Rider Alerts, GTFS-realtime/JSON/XML feeds to Transit, Google Maps, etc. Campus blog (8 Sep 2026): accurate real-time info is the #1 ridership factor; CU Boulder cut "ghost bus" complaints 75% after proactive disruption alerts. Pricing quote-only. **Copy its playbook** — multi-source GPS, disruption-aware ETAs, publish GTFS-rt — rather than fear it as an app.
- **TripSpark MyRide:** web + mobile app, point-to-point trip planner (Google-powered), live vehicle map, real-time schedules, departure notifications (text/email/push), SMS + IVR for riders without internet, agency branding, passenger-load data via API, WCAG 2.1 AA. Vendor claims +30% average vehicle-departure accuracy. No verified university/campus deployment and no NYU presence found; quote-only. Strong rider features, no campus footprint.
- **GMV Syncromatics:** founded 2006, acquired by GMV 2015; cloud CAD/AVL + real-time passenger information for 100+ US transit properties incl. LADOT. Campus white-label apps: TU Tiger Ride (Towson), UD Shuttle (Delaware), Campus Shuttle (CU Anschutz), CAT Prowler (North Dakota / Grand Forks). Real-time arrivals, live map, favourite stops, reminders, alerts; GTFS/GTFS-RT feeds out to Google Maps, Apple Maps, and Transit. Falls short in practice — Towson Play reviews mirror Passio's: crashes when clearing notifications, "shuttle times also sometimes don't track", "more than half the time it doesn't work or gives you inaccurate information", stops misplaced, won't open on poor reception. Cal Poly Pomona paid **$102,000+** for Bronco Express tracking that the student paper reports was "unreliable or non-existent" since 2020; contract runs to 15 Jul 2027, ~$16k/yr service fee. Free to riders; agency pricing not public (the Cal Poly figures are the only public price points found).
- **DoubleMap:** live GPS bus tracking; Indiana University, Georgetown, Univ. of Cincinnati, CityBus Lafayette, Bloomington Transit, Columbia (MO), Univ. of Akron Roo Express. Live buses on map, routes + stops, stop search, system announcements (delays/reroutes), web access. No trip planner, no favourites, no capacity found. Roughly par or worse than Passio: a Univ. of Southern Indiana student paper reports "delayed signal… following the bus as opposed to moving with the bus" and buses not appearing in real time — the same disappearing-bus complaint. Simpler UI is its only edge. Google Play 3.64, 7,112 ratings, 100K+ installs; iOS 4.01, 2.9K ratings. Free to riders; agency pricing not public.
- **Ride Systems: discontinued.** RideSystems app discontinued 31 Aug 2025 in favour of the TransLoc Rider app (Brownsville Metro notice, 25 Aug 2025); main app removed from Google Play 11 Oct 2025; UNO Shuttle Tracker removed 17 Oct 2025. Reviews before removal: "Doesn't work half the time. Now it doesn't even show where the busses are." A cautionary tale, not a threat — its demise shows Modaxo consolidating legacy campus vendors into TransLoc. Bussin's vendor-competitor set is shrinking to Passio vs TransLoc vs white-labels.
- **RouteMatch:** cloud transit-management software (Atlanta), mainly paratransit/NEMT + fixed-route scheduling, dispatch, real-time tracking, reporting/audits. No dedicated rider mobile app found (secondary listings say desktop/web only; treat as unverified). Quote-based; no public price. Not comparable as a rider experience.
- **Ecolane:** demand-response / paratransit / NEMT scheduling + dispatch, 300+ agencies per Capterra; campus-mobility blog pitches fixed-route + on-demand campus use. Overlaps Passio only on on-demand (Safe Ride-like) service. Listings derived from vendor data put starting price at **$50,000/yr** (G2/GetApp); a secondary 2026 paratransit ranking claims "from $4,500/mo" — **conflicts with the $50k/yr listing; do not rely on either without a vendor quote.**
- **Via:** on-demand shared-ride software (NYC-founded 2012) sold to cities/agencies/universities; riders book, algorithm groups them into shared vans. Campus examples: NU Transit (Northwestern, Via) — shuttle trip planning + Safe Ride booking, real-time ride tracking, ETA before booking (Google Play 4.4, 11 reviews; iOS 4.7 in an AppRecs sample of 114); Terp Ride (Univ. of Maryland, Via) — NITE Ride + paratransit booking, free, track reserved vehicle in real time. Not a fixed-route tracker — it solves the *other* NYU problem (overnight Safe Ride, currently a separate app/booking flow). Relevant only if Bussin ever adds on-demand night rides.

### 4.7 Passio weaknesses — cross-competitor scorecard

| Passio weakness | Who fixes it | Who shares it |
|---|---|---|
| Inaccurate / jumping ETAs | Transit (ML + GO crowdsourcing), Swiftly (20–50% claim, disruption-aware), XRadar (machine-learned, 1–2 min claim), RUBus (server-side recalculation), TripSpark (+30% claim) | TransLoc, DoubleMap, Syncromatics (Towson), Moovit (GPS lag reports) |
| Buses disappearing from map | Swiftly Auto-Assigner (matches vehicle to trip on hardware/login failure); Transit GO (crowdsourced position when official data missing) | Passio itself (store reviews), DoubleMap (delayed GPS), TransLoc (fails to track) |
| Crashes | XRadar / RUBus / Navgic (native, small, focused) — no crash pattern in their small review sets | TransLoc (3.2), Syncromatics/Tiger Ride (crashes clearing notifications), Transit (recent crash reports) |
| Confusing UI | Transit, XRadar, PennTransit, Moovit (step-by-step) | Passio (20 min to plan a transfer, per review), DoubleMap, Syncromatics ("hard to tell where the bus is") |
| No origin-to-destination trip planning | Transit, Moovit, Google Maps, MTA app, TransLoc, TripSpark MyRide, PennTransit | Passio, DoubleMap, Ride Systems, Syncromatics (no trip planner found) |
| Next-bus-only schedules | Transit/Moovit/Google (full timetables + offline), PennTransit (stop-by-stop schedules), XRadar (stops in order, all incoming buses) | Passio (one ETA at a time, per review) |
| Broken favourites / agency saving | Transit, Moovit, TransLoc, Syncromatics, XRadar (favourite routes/stops that persist) | Passio (kicked out of agency, SCAD review) |
| Capacity | Passio itself has it; also TransLoc new app, XRadar (tap bus), Swiftly-fed apps | Google Maps (crowding, MTA only), Moovit (crowding reports) |

### 4.8 Displacement pattern

Passio usually wins: it displaced TransLoc at Rutgers (2 Aug 2023), UConn (Dec 2021), NC State (12 Aug 2024), SMU, UChicago (2024), and Longwood Collective, Boston (2 Jan 2026). The one verified reverse is **Georgia Tech, July 2022 — TransLoc replaced Passio GO**, consolidating Stinger + on-demand Stingerette in one app. Rutgers has since moved to TripShot as official tracker (date not verified). Harvard moved Passio → Citymapper (1 Jul 2026); Rochester/RIT moved Passio → TripShot (fall 2025).

## 5. Openings for Bussin (compiled across all four passes)

1. **Shuttle-first simplicity + optional subway handoff.** Citymapper is broad but overwhelming ("20 different buses… a while to scroll to the M2"); TripShot/Passio are narrow but city-blind. Win on NYU shuttle depth, not city breadth — Citymapper already covers NYC transit.
2. **Trustworthy delay / cancellation handling with a stated reason.** Both rivals fail here in the field — vanishing/cancelled buses, uncommunicated route changes (TripShot: Michigan Tech route change with no message; Play reviews report cancelled shuttles that "just disappear"). A cancellation that says why beats a bus that silently vanishes.
3. **Consistent live capacity.** Passio already has it; TripShot matches via APC counts; Citymapper lacks it. Keep it, make it reliable.
4. **No-account, no-ads, web-first access.** Student-built RIT/Rutgers trackers prove the demand. Transit's Royale paywall ($5/mo or $25/yr unsponsored) is the opening: Bussin is free and shuttle-only.
5. **True campus-native customisation.** NYU naming, NYU stops, and SafeRide in one list — the shape Navgic already proves ("campus map + tracker combo is the expected shape"), and PennTransit is the gold standard for a university-built planner.
6. **A data-feed strategy, not just a nicer UI.** Harvard's student-led replacement was scrapped partly over hardware difficulty; Rochester's gap was unequipped substitute buses. A prettier UI alone inherits Passio's accuracy problem — the ETA engine is the moat. Copy Swiftly's playbook (multi-source GPS, disruption-aware ETAs, publish GTFS-realtime) and RUBus's (recalculate ETAs server-side from Passio positions).
7. **Match XRadar's ETA accuracy and favourites on day one.** It is the only rival already claiming NYU, rated 4.79, with reviews explicitly comparing itself favourably to Passio GO. Bussin can out-execute it on NYU depth, Android/web, and Safe Ride integration.
8. **Trip planning is table stakes.** Every app that beats Passio has origin-to-destination planning; Passio's lack of it is the single most repeated feature complaint. Include shuttle-vs-subway comparison and total trip time — the exact gap NYU students named ("Some days I wait over 30 minutes for a shuttle, only to realize I could have taken the train").
